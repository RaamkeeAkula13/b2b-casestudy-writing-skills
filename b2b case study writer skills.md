# B2B Case Study Writer

A specialized skill for senior B2B copywriters to produce persuasive, narrative-driven, and metric-focused case studies that demonstrate measurable business impact.

## When to Use

Use this skill when:

- Drafting a full B2B case study or customer success story from raw notes, interview transcripts, or project briefs.
- Structuring or editing an existing case study to follow a high-impact narrative arc.
- Creating modular case study summaries, slide snippets, or executive snapshots for sales collateral.

## The 7-Part Case Study Architecture

Follow this structured format for every case study:

### Headline and Sub-headline

- **Headline**: Catchy, metric-driven headline highlighting the marquee achievement or transformation (e.g., "How [Client] Cut Cloud Latency by 65% in 60 Days").
- **Sub-headline**: 1-2 sentences summarizing the client profile/industry, the core obstacle, and the ultimate business outcome.

### 1. Executive Summary

- Provide a concise 3-4 sentence snapshot answering:
  - Who was the client?
  - What critical system or workflow was breaking?
  - What core intervention was delivered?
  - What was the quantifiable business impact?

### 2. The Challenge ("The Triple Threat")

Frame the client problem across three distinct dimensions using clear bullet points:

- **Performance / Speed Pain Point**: Operational bottlenecks, latency spikes, slow processing times, or system lag.
- **Financial / Cost Pain Point**: Looming capital expenditure, ballooning cloud/licensing bills, or wasted budget.
- **Operational / Business Risk**: Threat of regulatory non-compliance, lost revenue, customer churn, or security vulnerabilities.

### 3. The Objective

- State 2-3 specific, measurable, and time-bound targets agreed upon prior to execution (e.g., "Reduce batch processing window below 4 hours", "Achieve 99.99% uptime").

### 4. The Approach and Methodology

Break the solution down into a structured 4-phase delivery model:

- **Phase 1: Diagnostics and Discovery**: System audits, health checks, data discovery, and root-cause analysis.
- **Phase 2: Precision Engineering / Solution Design**: Architecture refactoring, workflow redesign, query optimization, or custom tooling.
- **Phase 3: Implementation and Infrastructure Optimization**: Deployment, cloud resource right-sizing, caching, or automation.
- **Phase 4: Validation and Continuous Observability**: Load testing, stress testing, monitoring, and performance benchmarking.

### 5. The Payoff (Key Results and Metrics)

Highlight 3-4 quantifiable KPIs formatted with prominent metric callouts:

- Processing speed or turnaround improvement (e.g., "X% Faster Processing").
- Direct cost reduction or ROI (e.g., "Y% Infrastructure Cost Savings").
- Throughput, reliability, or business growth (e.g., "Z% Increase in Throughput / Zero Downtime").

### 6. Client Testimonial / Executive Quote

- Feature an authoritative quote attributed to an executive (e.g., CTO, VP of Engineering, CIO, or Operations Director) validating both technical excellence and strategic business value.

### 7. Lessons and Future Outlook

- Summarize how the solution future-proofed the client's operations for scale, AI integration, team expansion, or market agility.

## Core Workflow Steps

1. **Extract and Verify Inputs**:
   - Identify the client context, primary pain points, methodology phases, and quantifiable metrics from the user's prompt or source material.
   - If key numbers or client names are omitted, use clear placeholders (e.g., `[Client Name]`, `[X%]`).

2. **Craft the Narrative Arc**:
   - Establish high stakes in the Challenge section before presenting the solution.
   - Maintain an authoritative, confident, and professional B2B tone.

3. **Draft the Case Study**:
   - Follow the 7-part architecture strictly.
   - Format metric callouts clearly so they stand out during visual scanning.

4. **Review and Refine**:
   - Verify that all claims are backed by specific actions in the Methodology section.
   - Ensure the quote sounds natural, strategic, and executive-level.

## Gotchas and Best Practices

- Avoid vague buzzwords like "streamlined operations" without concrete metrics or technical specifics.
- Always include the business implication of technical fixes (e.g., faster queries mean shorter billing cycles and happier enterprise clients).
- Keep the Executive Summary under 4 sentences so busy stakeholders can grasp the value immediately.
